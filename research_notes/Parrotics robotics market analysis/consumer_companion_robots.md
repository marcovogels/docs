# Consumer companion, desktop and pet robots: market, products, failures (as of 2026-10-24)

Method note: compiled from web searches on 2026-10-24. Several primary sites (IEEE Spectrum, Axios, moxierobots.com) could not be fetched directly from the research environment, so some claims rest on search-result snippets of those pages. These are flagged "(snippet)". Many price/sales figures come from vendor blogs (notably keyirobot.com, run by Loona's maker, a competitor of Emo/Vector/Aibo). Treat those as lower reliability. Sources dated before 2025 are flagged "(older)".

## 1. Products, prices, launch dates, units sold, status in 2026

### Takeaway
The survivors are cheap desk robots ($100-$450: Eilik, Emo, Moflin, Loona at the upper end) sold through Kickstarter and Amazon, plus a few premium Japanese "pets" (Aibo, Lovot) that need subscriptions. Volumes are small. The best-documented current seller, Loona, reports roughly 90,000-130,000 units. Moflin has sold 20,000+ units in Japan. The big-tech home robots (Samsung Ballie, Amazon Astro) either never shipped or stayed invite-only.

### Inferences
- Units and prices sort products into tiers. The $100-$450 band (Eilik, Emo, Moflin, Loona, Ropet) is where volume is happening. The best-documented volumes are in the tens of thousands to low hundreds of thousands. No independent company has shown sustained million-unit annual sales since Anki.

## 2. Failure analysis: why Anki, Jibo, Embodied/Moxie and others failed

### Takeaway
Almost every failure combines three things: a high upfront hardware cost with no recurring revenue, dependence on the company's own cloud (so a shutdown bricks the product), and engagement that fades after the novelty wears off. Anki and Embodied died when a single late-stage financing fell through. Jibo was too late, too expensive, and undercut by $50-$180 smart speakers.

### Cited Findings
- **Anki:**
  - It died when a late-stage strategic investment fell through — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/29/cozmo-maker-anki-is-shutting-its-doors)
  - It had about $100M in 2017 revenue but no subscription-style recurring revenue, and "obviously ran out of money". Prices of $180-$249 likely slowed uptake. — [ProductMint](https://productmint.com/what-happened-to-anki/)
  - It burned nearly $200M — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/robotics-startup-anki-shuts-down-after-burning-through-almost-200-million)
- **Vector after Anki:** buyers were left with robots dependent on dead cloud servers, and DDL had to crowdfund to remove that dependence — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/digital-dream-labs-will-revive-shuttered-startup-ankis-vector-robot); [Byteside (older)](https://www.byteside.com/2019/07/anki-vector-dying-what-can-you-do-choice-consumer-law-australia/)
- **DDL:** taking preorders and failing to fulfill them led to a state lawsuit and reputational collapse — [PA AG](https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/?p=30224)
- **Jibo:**
  - It shipped late, after many delays, at $899, while Amazon priced Echo about 75% lower — [ideaproof](https://ideaproof.io/failure/jibo); [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/jibo-social-robot-analyzing-what-went-wrong/)
  - Reviewers criticized limited skills and voice recognition weaker than Alexa's. The company ran out of money before it could iterate. — [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/jibo-social-robot-analyzing-what-went-wrong/)
  - Its server shutdown ended functionality — [Liliputing](https://liliputing.com/jibos-robot-shaped-smart-speaker-becomes-even-more-useless-as-servers-are-shut-off/)
  - Commentators point to the lack of a "killer app" or real need, and to technology not ready for the promised experience at an affordable price — [IEEE Spectrum (snippet)](https://spectrum.ieee.org/why-the-pursuit-of-a-killer-app-for-home-robots-is-fraught-with-peril); [IEEE Spectrum – Anki, Jibo, Kuri lessons (snippet)](https://spectrum.ieee.org/amp/anki-jibo-and-kuri-what-we-can-learn-from-social-robotics-failures-2650278469)
- **Embodied/Moxie:**
  - A lead investor withdrew from a vital round — [Axios (snippet)](https://www.axios.com/2024/12/10/moxie-kids-robot-shuts-down)
  - Conversations ran on remote LLMs, so when the company stopped paying the bills, the robots stopped working — [Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/technology/moxie-robot-offline/); [Daily Dot](https://dailydot.com/moxie-ai-robot-shutting-down)
  - It offered no refunds on the $799 robot. Its demise forced children to deal with the "death" of a companion, which drew emotional backlash. — [HotHardware](https://hothardware.com/news/ai-robot-maker-pulls-the-plug)
  - It prompted calls for robot end-of-life standards — [Six Degrees of Robotics](https://sixdegreesofrobotics.substack.com/p/what-happens-when-robots-die-the)
- **Amazon Astro for Business:** killed after seven months. The devices were remotely disabled, and Amazon gave refunds plus credit, showing a big company's cost of exit. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/03/amazon-discontinues-astro-for-business-security-robot)
- **Samsung Ballie:** delayed repeatedly; reports say Samsung struggled to define its place in the home ("identity crisis") — [heise](https://heise.de/-10476416); [Android Central](https://androidcentral.com/accessories/smart-home/samsung-ballie-another-setback-concerns-home-use-report)
- **Aibo:** a subscription is needed for full function, and features freeze if it lapses. A cloud lifecycle risk remains even for Sony. — [Robozaps](https://blog.robozaps.com/b/sony-aibo-review)

### Inferences (lessons for a bird-robot newcomer)
- **Design for company death.** Core personality, sound mimicry and learned movements should run locally. Publish an offline or open-server fallback, as OpenMoxie and the DDL Vector work did after the fact. This also becomes a marketing point after the Moxie backlash.
- **Avoid paying per-user LLM inference indefinitely on a one-time sale.** Either cap cloud features behind an optional subscription or use on-device or small models. Emo's on-device accelerator is an example.
- **Keep BOM and price in the $100-$300 impulse band.** Every failure above was priced at $799-$899 (Moxie, Jibo) or relied on heavy VC for $180-$249 robots with thin margins (Anki).
- **Don't depend on one mega-round.** Anki (~$200M) and Embodied (~$88M, unverified) both died on a single financing. Capital-light Kickstarter-to-Amazon companies (Energize Lab, Living AI, KEYi) survived.
- **Fulfill preorders.** DDL's preorder failures led to legal action.
- **Address novelty wear-off.** The learning loops in the Parrotics concept (sound mimicry, teaching movements by demonstration) are a plausible retention mechanism. No retention data was found to confirm this.

### Gaps
- No published retention or engagement curves (e.g., daily-active rates after 30/90 days) for any companion robot.
- No BOM or margin data was found for any product.
- No direct postmortem from Embodied's founders was retrieved.

## 3. Successes: what sells and why (price points, channels, margins, subscription vs one-off)

### Inferences
- The proven playbook is a Kickstarter launch at $99-$300 with roughly a $100K-$1M raise, then Amazon/DTC with holiday promotions and line extensions (colors, mini versions such as Eiliko, Deskmate). A Kickstarter raise in the $0.4-3M range looks typical for a successful launch. It validates demand at the thousands-of-units level, not the mass market.
- TikTok/short-video virality is widely assumed to drive Eilik/Emo/Loona sales, but I found no source quantifying it (see gaps).

### Gaps

## 5. Trends: LLMs, on-device vs cloud, privacy, regulation, crowdfunding track record

### Takeaway
LLM conversation is now standard in new companions, but 2025-26 showed its risks with children. The FoloToy Kumma teddy was pulled, Mattel/OpenAI delayed its toy, and US senators demanded transparency. The EU now treats AI-enabled toys as high-risk under the AI Act, and the new Toy Safety Regulation is in force with a transition period to 2030. Cloud dependence is a recognized consumer-protection issue.

### Cited Findings
- **FoloToy Kumma ($99, GPT-4o-based):** US PIRG testing (Nov 13, 2025) found it discussing sexual topics and where to find knives and matches. FoloToy suspended its entire AI toy range for a safety audit. OpenAI reportedly suspended FoloToy, though only one source says so. — [CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/sci-tech/article/sales-of-ai-enabled-teddy-bear-suspended-after-it-gave-advice-on-bdsm-sex-and-where-to-find-knives/); [OECD AI incident](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents/2025-11-15-2080)
- **Mattel/OpenAI:** partnership announced June 2025; first toy delayed in December 2025 amid safety scrutiny. Mattel reportedly pivoted toward older audiences, in line with OpenAI's age limits. Sens. Blackburn and Blumenthal wrote to AI toy makers demanding transparency. — [PIRG](https://pirg.org/media-center/statement-mattel-openai-delay-ai-product-launch-as-senators-demand-transparency-from-toy-companies/); [Fairplay](https://fairplayforkids.org/statement-on-mattel-delaying-release-of-its-first-toy-with-openai/); [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/mattel-delays-openai-toy-launch-amid-safety-scrutiny/)
- **EU:**
  - The Toy Safety Regulation (EU) 2025/2509 entered into force on Jan 1, 2026, with a transition to Aug 1, 2030. It requires assessing connected and AI toys for children's specific vulnerabilities (possibly including mental health), includes privacy safeguards for toys that process personal data, and adds a Digital Product Passport.
  - AI in toys is treated as high-risk under the AI Act (Reg. 2024/1689), which brings risk management, logging and human oversight. Commentators say the TSR is weak on conversational companions. Verify against the official text.
  - Sources: [medialaws.eu](https://www.medialaws.eu/talking-toys-ai-companions-childrens-safety-and-data-and-the-limits-of-the-new-eu-toy-safety-regulation/); [medialaws.eu – EU vs US](https://www.medialaws.eu/playing-by-the-rules-governing-ai-toys-in-the-european-union-and-the-united-states/); [PLMJ guide](https://www.plmj.com/xms/files/07_Guias_e_Manuais/2026/Toy_safety_regulation.pdf); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/luka-kids-ai-drawing-projector-ifa-2026-eu-toy-safety-regulation-2025-2509-mental-health-ai-act)
- **UK:** call for evidence on toy safety regulations published July 6, 2026 — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/calls-for-evidence/toys-safety-regulations-call-for-evidence/toys-safety-regulations-call-for-evidence)
- **China:** only 32.1% of families have usage guidelines for children's AI companions (2025 survey). Experts warn about effects on children's emotional development. — [KuCoin news summary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/2025-china-youth-survey-over-60-of-students-use-ai-risks-of-ai-companions-for-children-emerge)
- **Privacy and ethics** concerns were raised about CES 2026 home and companion robots — [KontraNet](https://kontranet.com/future-of-iot/ai-home-companion-robots-2026/)
- **On-device compute is appearing.** Emo uses a neural accelerator of about 1.2 TOPS — [Hackster](https://hackster.io/news/living-ai-s-emo-pet-uses-1-2-tops-of-neural-compute-performance-to-liven-up-your-desk-cc42a5372c04)
- **Crowdfunding track record is mixed.**
  - Jibo: Indiegogo delays and refunds — [ideaproof](https://ideaproof.io/failure/jibo)
  - DDL: unfulfilled preorders — [PA AG](https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/?p=30224)
  - Eilik, Loona and Ropet campaigns delivered and led to retail — [originofbots](https://www.originofbots.com/news/eilik-enters-mass-production-after-kickstarter-surge); [Gasgoo](https://autonews.gasgoo.com/articles/market-industry/seeds-keyi-technology-raises-nearly-rmb-100-million-in-new-funding-round-2008898565877620737)

### Inferences
- A sound-mimicking bird robot that records audio and learns from demonstration processes voice (personal data). If it is marketed to children, it falls under COPPA in the US and under the TSR and AI Act in the EU. Positioning it as an adult/teen desk companion, keeping audio processing on-device and avoiding open-ended LLM chat for kids would lower regulatory and PR risk.
- A parrot's mimicry is a "safe" interaction mode. It repeats and remixes the user's own sounds instead of generating open-ended content, which avoids the Kumma-style failure.

## 6. Existing bird/parrot-shaped robot products and how they did

### Takeaway
I found no successful, well-known consumer bird or parrot companion robot. The space consists of cheap record-and-repeat plush parrot toys ($15-$25 on Walmart/Amazon), kids' toys like ZURU Pets Alive "Polly the Magic Bird", a Kickstarter elder-care robot parrot (Onorato AI) with unclear outcome, and a beta AI plush parrot (ChatPET). This is both an opportunity (no incumbent) and a warning (no proof of demand at premium prices).
