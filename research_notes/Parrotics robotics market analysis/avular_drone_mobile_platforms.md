## 1. Avular: what it does, founding, founders, funding, headcount, products, software, customers, partnerships, recent news

### Takeaway
Avular (Avular Innovations B.V., Eindhoven) was founded in 2014 as a TU/e spin-off. It sells two ROS 2 research and development platforms: the Vertex One drone and the Origin One ground robot. Its growth story has shifted toward "applied autonomy": co-developing autonomy for industrial OEMs and operators such as Heijmans (asphalt roller), SGL (golf-green robot) and i-team (cleaning). It is backed mainly by one investor, the Dutch family holding Lumipol. Disclosed outside funding is small, a few million euros, which sets it far apart from the defence-drone unicorns. It markets itself on European, in-house and independent technology.

### Inferences
- Avular's model is a hybrid: low-volume research platforms (Vertex One, Origin One) plus higher-value co-development contracts. The applied-autonomy pages now get the most prominent placement. That suggests the research-platform business alone may not be enough to sustain about 100 staff.
- With roughly €2-4M of disclosed outside equity and one dominant investor, Avular is capital-light compared with defence-drone peers that raise hundreds of millions of euros. A well-funded entrant could outspend it, but Avular has about 12 years of in-house autopilot and integration IP, a 6,500 m² facility and reference customers.
- The "Designed and built in the Netherlands" messaging and the "independence" award show that Avular is positioning on European sovereignty. That is the same opening a new entrant would be targeting.
- Avular's site does not mention defence. The Helmut-Schmidt-Universität (Bundeswehr university) swarm customer and the drone-sector BD hire are weak signals of possible dual-use interest. This is speculative.

## 2. Competitors in research and development platforms: prices, segments, business models

### Takeaway
The research and development platform market splits into four groups:
- Cheap Chinese ROS ground robots and quadrupeds: AgileX at about $3-5K, Unitree Go2 EDU at about $11K.
- Premium North American UGVs: Clearpath, now owned by Rockwell, with Husky A300 and Jackal.
- NDAA-compliant US development drones: ModalAI Starling 2 at about $3K.
- Small European open-source niche makers: Bitcraze Crazyflie at about $480, Flapper Nimble+ at about $2,200.

The well-capitalised European players (Parrot, Auterion, Quantum Systems and others) have mostly pivoted to defence and ISR rather than research kits.

## 3. Market size and growth: commercial drones, research and education drones, mobile robot platforms, defence drones

### Takeaway
Commercial drones are about a $40B market (2025), heading to about $58B by 2030 by DII's count. Hardware is only about $7B of that; services dominate. AMR and mobile-robot forecasts vary by a factor of four between analysts. No credible source sizes the "research and education platform" niche, which is probably a few hundred million dollars at most (inference). The real growth and capital are in European defence drones. European defence startups raised $8.7B in 2025, and Dealroom forecasts $10.5B for 2026, with drones and autonomy at about 74% of EU funding.

### Cited Findings
- **Drone Industry Insights (DII):**
  - Commercial drone market: $40.6B in 2025, rising to $57.8B in 2030.
  - 2025 split: services $29.4B, hardware $6.7B, software $1.7B.
  - Hardware is the fastest-growing segment.
  - Sources: [DII project page](https://droneii.com/project/drone-market-size-and-growth-2025-2030); [DII blog](https://droneii.com/drone-market-growth-in-2025-and-beyond) (search summary)
  - A report reseller gives a CAGR of about 7.9%, energy as the top vertical, and Asia as the largest region. — [GII listing](https://giikorea.co.kr/report/dro1676294-global-drone-market-report-drone-market-size.html)
  - Reliability: DII is a specialist analyst and the most credible of these. The full report is paywalled.
- Other drone totals are not comparable: IMARC gives $33.9B for 2025, and Grand View gives about $83.8B for 2025, with different scopes (search summary). — [DII search context](https://droneii.com/project/drone-market-size-and-growth-2025-2030)

  - Dealroom forecasts a record $10.5B in 2026. Drones and autonomous systems are expected to take about 74% of EU defence funding and 77% in the UK, against 57% in the US.
  - Source: [Defense News, 5 Oct 2026](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/10/05/european-defense-startups-to-raise-record-105-billion-in-2026-dealroom-forecasts/) (search summary)
- **Policy money:**
  - EU SAFE is a €150B loan instrument that names drones and counter-drone as priorities. EDIP calls support production scale-up.
  - The European Drone Defence Initiative (the "drone wall") is a flagship of the Defence Readiness Roadmap 2030 (Oct 2025). Initial capability is due by end-2026 and full capability by end-2027. No dedicated budget figure was found.
  - Sources: [EC Readiness Roadmap 2030](https://defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu/eu-defence-industry/readiness-roadmap-2030_en); [EPRS briefing](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/777962/EPRS_ATA(2025)777962_EN.pdf); [DroneLife, Oct 2025](https://dronelife.com/2025/10/17/european-drone-defense-initiative/)

### Inferences
- The commercial hardware pool is about $6.7B globally, and China dominates it. Research and education platforms are a small slice of that. A startup that sells only research kits is chasing a small, slow-growing niche, though one with high margins.
- Capital and growth are concentrated in defence and dual-use autonomy. Any "Parrotics" funding story would likely need a credible dual-use angle to access the 2026 European funding wave.

### Gaps
- No credible sizing for research and education drones or for mobile-robot development platforms specifically.
- No Gartner figures were found.
- Defence market numbers come mainly from MarketsandMarkets, whose figures are inconsistent. Primary NATO or EU budget data was not gathered.

## 4. Regulatory environment (EU and US) and openings for European makers

### Takeaway
In the EU, EASA C-class marking and Direct Remote ID add certification cost but apply equally to everyone. In the US, the regulatory change has been dramatic, and it cuts both ways. Since 22 Dec 2025 the FCC blocks all new foreign-made drone models and critical components, not just Chinese ones, unless they are Blue UAS listed, Buy-American compliant, or individually cleared by the Department of War or DHS. On 28 Jul 2026 the FCC added foreign-made "advanced robotic devices" to the same list: humanoids, quadrupeds and mobile robots. So a European maker gains in Europe from sovereignty-driven buying, but now faces a US market that is closed by default to new foreign drone and robot models.

### Cited Findings
- **FCC drone listing:**
  - On 22 Dec 2025 the FCC added foreign-produced UAS and critical components to the Covered List. New models cannot get equipment authorisation, so they cannot be imported, marketed or sold in the US.
  - Already-authorised models are unaffected. The firmware waiver runs to at least 1 Jan 2029.
  - The legal basis is FY25 NDAA §1709.
  - Sources: [DroneLife, 22 Dec 2025](https://dronelife.com/2025/12/22/fcc-adds-foreign-made-drones-and-components-to-covered-list-citing-national-security-risks/); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/12/23/business/us-ban-foreign-drones-dji-intl-hnk); [FCC fact sheet](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-423277A1.pdf)
- "DJI wasn't singled out. Instead, the FCC has added any new… foreign‑made drones and certain drone components… unless they get a special exemption." — [UAV Coach, updated Jun 2026](https://uavcoach.com/dji-ban/)
- **Exemptions:**
  - 7 Jan 2026: a Department of War determination exempted Blue UAS Cleared List items and Buy American "domestic end products". — [DroneLife, 7 Jan 2026](https://dronelife.com/2026/01/07/cc-covered-list-blue-uas-buy-american-exemptions-2027/); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/01/fcc-exempts-certain-drones-and-components-from-covered-list-to-address-national-security-risks)
  - Mar 2026: the first case-by-case Conditional Approvals, for four UAS. — [ExecutiveGov](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/fcc-drones-conditional-approval-dow)
  - May 2026: more approvals, including Elevon Aerial and Air6. — [DroneLife, May 2026](https://dronelife.com/2026/05/18/cc-covered-list-drone-exemptions-elevon-air6/)
  - 21 Jul 2026: the Blue UAS and Buy American exemptions were extended to 1 Jan 2028, and the expiry for Conditional Approvals was removed. The notice also sought comment on banning imports of foreign military-grade drones. — [FCC DA 26-758](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-758A1.pdf)
  - All via search summary.
- DJI asked the FCC to reconsider (Jan 2026) and appealed to the Ninth Circuit. As of Oct 2026 the listing was reported still in place (search summary). — [UAV Coach](https://uavcoach.com/dji-ban/)
- **FCC robot listing:**
  - On 28 Jul 2026 (DA 26-786) the FCC added foreign-produced "advanced robotic devices" to the Covered List. Reports describe this as covering humanoids, quadrupeds and mobile robots.
  - New models need a Department of War Conditional Approval. Previously authorised models (for example, Unitree Go2's 2024 grant) are grandfathered.
  - Sources conflict on whether Unitree is named.
  - Sources: [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2026/china-robot-ban-silicon-valley/); [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/321890/20260728/fcc-bans-chinese-humanoid-robots-power-inverters-linked-confirmed-backdoors.htm); [FAI analysis](https://www.thefai.org/posts/did-the-fcc-just-ban-foreign-robots) (search summary)
  - Reliability: mostly secondary sources. Check the FCC DA 26-786 text.
- **EU rules:**
  - Regulation (EU) 2019/945 defines C0-C6 class marks. Direct Remote ID applied to class-marked drones from Jan 2024.
  - Privately built drones may fly in Open A3, or A1 if under 250 g, without a class mark.
  - Network Remote ID is being developed under U-space.
  - Sources are mostly vendor and blog guides, not EASA texts: [Spilma guide](https://www.spilma.com/en/guides/easa-c0-c6-drone-categories); [AirHub](https://www.airhub.app/resources/news/remote-id-easa-vs-uk-2026)
- Avular explicitly markets EU design and production as beneficial because of "security-concerns over foreign delicate technology". — [Avular Drones](https://www.avular.com/drones)

### Inferences
- Openings in Europe: sovereignty buying (public sector, utilities, defence-adjacent research), EU defence funding through SAFE and EDIP, and DJI-replacement demand. Parrot's 2025-26 growth shows these openings are real.
- The US is now a closed door by default for a Dutch or European startup's new drone or robot models. The ways in are Blue UAS listing (slow and costly), US manufacturing to qualify under Buy American, or a DoW/DHS Conditional Approval. This applies to Avular as well. Its MicroVision MoU and US ambitions would run into this rule for any new model.
- Research platforms that are custom-built or used under the Specific category may avoid C-class certification in the EU. But selling a drone "placed on the market" for Open-category use needs a class mark and CE conformity.

### Gaps
- The EASA primary regulation text was not fetched. Exact Remote ID applicability by class differs between secondary sources.
- Not determined: whether the FCC robot listing covers research UGVs like Origin One or Husky, or only certain categories. Also not checked: whether any EU-allied exemption track exists.

## 5. Entry barriers: capital, certification, supply chain, DJI, dual-use and export

### Takeaway
The barriers are moderate for a research-kit niche and very high for scale drone manufacturing:
- Chinese dominance of components: China controls about 80-90% of the drone market and supplies most motors, batteries and flight controllers. China began restricting component exports to the US and Europe in late 2024.
- Certification for the US (Blue UAS) and the EU (C-class/CE, plus Specific-category operational approvals).
- Incumbents with years of autopilot IP.
- Dual-use export controls once products drift toward defence.

### Cited Findings
- Late 2024: Chinese suppliers began limiting shipments of motors, batteries and flight controllers to the US and Europe. Beijing was moving toward formal export controls. China is estimated to control 80-90% of the global drone market. — [Bloomberg via BNN](https://bnnbloomberg.ca/business/international/2024/12/09/china-is-cutting-off-drone-supplies-critical-to-ukraine-war-effort); [DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/2024/12/30/china-drone-export-controls) (search summary)
- CEPA argues that breaking dependence on China's drone ecosystem "cannot be broken overnight" because of China's cost advantages. — [CEPA](https://cepa.org/article/reality-check-breaking-free-from-chinas-drone-ecosystem-is-harder-than-you-think/) (search summary)
- Even Skydio, with about $4.4B valuation, had to ration batteries to one per drone after Chinese sanctions, because batteries were still sourced from China. — [Skydio blog](https://www.skydio.com/blog/chinas-sanctions-on-skydio)
- Avular describes in-house hardware, software and AI as "slower and more expensive". It needed about 12 years, about 100 staff and a 6,500 m² facility to reach its current position. — [Avular Changemaker news](https://www.avular.com/news/albert-maas-wins-changemaker-award-2026); [Avular Applied Autonomy](https://www.avular.com/applied-autonomy)
- Capital benchmarks:
  - Skydio's Apr 2026 raise was $110M. — [Skydio](https://www.skydio.com/resources/podcasts/skydio-raises-usd110m-series-f-at-usd4-4b-valuation-ceo-adam-bry)
  - Auterion raised $130M in 2025. — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/auterion-raises-130-million-aims-to-be-microsoft-for-drones)
  - Parrot, with about €80M revenue, still lost €14.5M in 2025. — [Parrot FY2025](https://www.actusnews.com/en/parrot/pr/2026/03/27/parrot-2025-full-year-earnings)
- Open-source stacks lower the software barrier. ModalAI and others build on PX4. Avular supports QGroundControl, ROS 2, Gazebo and RViz. — [ModalAI on PX4 docs](https://docs.px4.io/main/uk/complete_vehicles_mc/modalai_starling.html); [Avular Robots](https://www.avular.com/robots)

### Inferences
- A research-kit startup can launch on PX4 or ArduPilot and ROS 2 with modest capital, likely low single-digit € millions, by reusing open-source components. Differentiating from AgileX and Unitree on price is not feasible. Differentiation has to come from a novel form factor, EU sovereignty or non-Chinese supply, or a specific capability.
- If products become dual-use (ISR, swarming), EU dual-use export controls (Reg. 2021/821) and national arms-export rules would apply. This is background knowledge, not researched here.

### Gaps
- No sourced figures on EU C-class certification cost or time, or Blue UAS listing cost.
- The current (2026) status of China's formal drone-component export rules was not confirmed.
- Dual-use and export-control specifics were not researched.

## 6. Notable gaps and niches (perching, indoor, bird-inspired and flapping, education kits, open-source autonomy)

### Takeaway
The clearest under-served niches are:
- Bio-inspired and flapping-wing research drones: Flapper Drones is essentially alone and still in "early access".
- Indoor and micro research drones from non-Chinese, NDAA/EU-sovereign suppliers: Bitcraze and ModalAI dominate, and both are small.
- Affordable European ROS education robots and drones: Chinese AgileX and Unitree dominate on price but are now blocked for new models in the US.
- "Autonomy-as-a-service" for OEMs, which is the space Avular is moving into.

No commercial perching-drone research platform was identified in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Flapper Nimble+ claims to be the "first, and so far the only hover-capable tailless flapping-wing drone available". It costs $2,200, is in early access, and targets universities, labs and drone shows. — [Hackster](https://hackster.io/news/flapper-drones-releases-its-latest-open-source-drone-the-flapper-nimble-0e743166b573); [Bitcraze store](https://store.bitcraze.io/products/flapper-nimble-starter-kit)

### Inferences
- A "Parrotics" entry focused on bird-inspired or perching drones would face little direct competition: Flapper does flapping wings, and no perching platform was found. But it would be selling into a very small research market. It would likely need a dual-use or inspection application, such as perch-and-stare ISR or long-endurance infrastructure monitoring, to reach venture scale.
- One niche combines the sovereignty trend with the education gap: a European-made ROS 2 education kit with a non-Chinese bill of materials, plus a curriculum and digital twin in the style of Quanser. Price competition from AgileX would remain fierce in the EU, where Chinese robots are not banned.
